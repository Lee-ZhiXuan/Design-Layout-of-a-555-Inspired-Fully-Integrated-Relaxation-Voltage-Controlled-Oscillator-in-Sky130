## Overview

A fully integrated, highly tuneable, relaxation-type voltage-controlled oscillator (VCO), designed from transistor level to layout on the open-source SkyWater 130 nm process node. The project follows a complete front-to-back analog IC design flow, from schematic capture, simulation, physical layout, to layout verification (DRC/LVS), using an entirely open-source toolchain: Xschem, Ngspice, and Magic VLSI.

The core idea borrows the classic 555 timer's relaxation topology and rebuilds it as a modern CMOS-only, temperature-compensated, voltage-tunable oscillator suitable as an on-chip MHz domain clock source for low-power IoT sensor nodes.

## Motivation

Deeply embedded IoT devices such as sensor nodes, biomedical implants, and environmental monitors run for months or years on a strict energy budget, yet still need reliable on-chip timing for sleep-wake scheduling, PWM, and clock generation. Conventional choices such as crystal oscillators are bulky, off-chip, and power-hungry at startup, which conflicts with the push toward fully integrated, miniaturized SoCs.

At the same time, most existing integrated timing circuits in the literature are either:
- Designed in proprietary foundry nodes, making them inaccessible and non-reproducible outside institutions with expensive licenses, or
- Over-engineered for frequency precision at the cost of power, area, and simplicity.

The emergence of the open-source Sky130 PDK removes the foundry-access barrier, making it possible to design, simulate, and even fabricate real silicon without institutional support. This project uses that opportunity to build a low-power, adaptable, fully open timing IP block while also serving as a personal deep-dive into practical analog/mixed-signal IC design.

## Aim

To implement and validate a low-power, fully-integrated, tuneable timing circuit in Sky130, demonstrating a complete front-to-back IC design flow (schematic → simulation → layout → verification) while analyzing trade-offs in power, area, and performance.

## Objectives

1. Design a low-power, tuneable timing circuit at transistor level in Sky130, targeting an output frequency in the MHz domain.
2. Develop a DRC- and LVS-clean physical layout, and validate performance post-layout including parasitic (PEX) effects.
3. Evaluate the circuit's performance at both schematic and post-layout level in terms of area, power dissipation, frequency performance, and resilience to PVT (process, voltage, temperature) variation, against the following targets:
   - Power dissipation **< 100 µW**
   - Active die area **< 0.01 mm²**
   - Operational temperature range **-40 °C to 125 °C**
   - Monte Carlo yield **> 80%**

## Solution

The VCO uses a relaxation architecture built around a single timing MiM capacitor that charges and discharges between two voltage thresholds, with those thresholds generated by a novel op-amp-based thresholding circuit rather than a fixed resistor-divider. This gives the design continuous, wide-range voltage control over the oscillation frequency, representing a significant improvement over the fixed or narrow-range tuning in traditional 555-based oscillators.

The circuit is broken into 6 blocks:

| Block | Function |
|---|---|
| **PTAT current source** | CMOS-only beta-multiplier circuit providing first-order temperature-compensated biasing to the whole design |
| **Op-amp (thresholding)** | Takes the external control voltage and generates the upper/lower comparator thresholds |
| **PMOS / NMOS input comparators** | Detect when the capacitor voltage crosses the upper/lower thresholds |
| **SR-latch** | Converts comparator outputs into a stable digital state that drives charge/discharge switching |
| **Charge/discharge network + buffer** | Switches the capacitor charge path and drives the final output with sufficient strength |

**Design flow:** Xschem (schematic capture) → ngspice (simulation) → Magic VLSI (physical layout) → DRC/LVS (layout verification), entirely on open-source tooling using the Sky130 PDK.
